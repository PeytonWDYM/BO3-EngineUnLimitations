Typed actor and sentient save-handle failures. Written before patch logic.

1. Reject a changed executable, native descriptor owner, switch table, frame, or compare window.
2. Native writers refuse actor handles above 64 and sentient handles above 104 before the repair.
3. Expanded writers and readers must preserve one-based actor handles through 500 and sentient handles through 540.
4. Legacy and VM-only readers must retain actor bound 64 and sentient bound 104.
5. Reject a zero encoded handle when the native raw pointer field is nonzero.
6. Retain the native null-field branch. It consumes no attachment bytes.
7. Refuse the first out-of-range handle before publishing a reconstructed pointer.
8. Refuse a pointer one record before the current pool on write.
9. Retain four-byte framing and the exact following section offset.
10. Decode against the current relocated pool after a saved-state or host-transfer roundtrip.
11. Preserve native record strides, nonvolatile registers, stack return, and untouched field bytes.
12. Retain paired immutable per-load counts and reset the legacy bounds after normal or error completion.
13. Keep ordinary entities, secondary pointer families, and their format policies unchanged.
14. Register every compare-stub instruction phase with its actual native owning frame.
15. Publish all compare hooks in the existing transaction. Refusal or rollback changes no source or helper bytes.

Native descriptor replays use explicit scalar-I/O and diagnostic endpoints.
These checks do not establish complete game saves, host migration, or peer compatibility.
