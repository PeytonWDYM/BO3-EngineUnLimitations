Pure-job freeze failures (written before job-freeze implementation)

Retain the process-state bypass and controller-death counterexamples as history.
Use only owned fixture targets. No debugger, game, Steam, or integration.

1. Job class 18 or its exact 16-byte contract is unavailable or rejected.
2. A native result is success but ordinary, hidden, waiting, churn, or existing
   0x40 bypass workers execute after the freeze call returns.
3. A new ordinary or 0x40 bypass thread enters its target routine while frozen.
4. Initial primary-only inventory changes. Do not infer arbitrary process safety
   from fixture counters or an initial one-thread inventory.
5. Thaw fails or workers remain frozen. Preserve raw NTSTATUS and observations.
6. Wrong process identity, wrong job membership, multiple job members, invalid
   handle, insufficient rights, or malformed size are accepted by the wrapper.
7. Last close of the noninheritable parent-only kill-on-close job leaves a live
   frozen child. Retain process-handle signaling and the raw exit code.
8. Abrupt controller death during a partial marker resumes any ordinary or
   bypass worker before the nested kill-on-close job terminates its target.
   Use external sentinels and repeat the case. No process-state handle or child
   state reference exists in the pure-job controller.
9. Nested job admission refuses. Stop before freezing or modifying the target.
   Actual Steam job hierarchy and restrictions remain outside this proof.
10. A failure leaves any owned target alive. The runner owns a separate outer
    kill-on-close job. Never inherit either job handle into the target.
11. Receipt omits exact PID, creation time, executable/native hashes, statuses,
    first-instruction counters, elapsed observation time, or raw exit status.
12. A dedicated tight user-mode worker executes after freeze returns. Stable
    waiting workers alone do not establish this case.

No production quiescence claim follows from these fixtures alone. Native API
semantics and thread-creation constraints still require independent review.
